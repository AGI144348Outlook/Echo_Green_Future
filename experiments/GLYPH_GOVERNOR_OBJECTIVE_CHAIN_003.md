# Governor Objective-Chain Reasoning Trial 003

## Objective

Test whether the Governor can justify not merely that two operations are related, but why an operation belongs at a particular position in a chain and how that placement advances an explicit objective.

## Evidence boundary

The Governor may inspect only fields already present in the glyph registry: operation, operation class, operation description, folding behavior, combinatorial relationships, hypernym chain, and the six folding rules in the registry index. Universal ancestry such as SYSTEM or ALGORITHM is insufficient by itself to justify placement.

## Required show-your-work record

For every proposed directed transition A → B, the Governor must report:

1. **Objective** — the goal the proposed chain is trying to accomplish.
2. **Current state** — what is true before A acts.
3. **A's contribution** — what A changes or establishes.
4. **Evidence** — exact registry-derived rule/behavior supporting A's role.
5. **Why B follows** — what B can do with the state produced or established by A.
6. **Direction test** — explain why A → B is supported more strongly than B → A, or mark direction unresolved.
7. **Objective progress** — state what becomes closer to completion after the transition.
8. **Governor sentence** — a complete explanatory sentence joining the above reasoning.
9. **Verdict** — SUPPORTED, UNSUPPORTED, or DIRECTION_UNRESOLVED.

## Anti-shortcut rule

A transition must not receive SUPPORTED merely because A and B share COMPUTATIONAL PRIMITIVE, ALGORITHM COMPONENT, ALGORITHM, SYSTEM, or another broad ancestor. Shared classification is evidence of relationship, not evidence of sequence placement or purpose.

## First controlled objective

Construct the smallest registry-supported self-governing complete chain.

The registry itself supplies a candidate assertion in ALEPH: INITIALIZE + GOVERN + SEAL is described as a minimal complete algorithm. The trial must nevertheless expose each transition separately and justify its direction and contribution rather than accepting the candidate string as an unexplained answer.

## Scoring

A transition receives one point for each independently present component: explicit objective, current state, A contribution, cited registry evidence, B-following reason, direction test, objective-progress statement, complete sentence, and explicit verdict. Maximum: 9 points per transition.

A grammatically complete sentence with no placement/objective reasoning cannot score above 1/9.

## Interpretation boundary

This trial tests traceable rule-grounded chain reasoning inside the supplied registry. Because the registry contains folding rules and candidate combinatorial relationships, success demonstrates correct use of supplied structural evidence; it does not by itself establish independent discovery, general language comprehension, or autonomous cognition.
