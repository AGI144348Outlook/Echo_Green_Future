## What the passage argues (paraphrase, not endorsed as fact)
The author distinguishes mathematical physical laws, finite discrete computations, and the interpretation used to turn simulation outputs into explanatory narratives. The 'inferential staircase' is the sequence of conceptual and computational intermediaries from physical law toward explanation, and the passage challenges whether those intermediaries preserve the simplicity attributed to foundational laws. This is a philosophical argument, not a proof that biology or mathematics is nonalgorithmic.

## Timothy's proposed bidirectional research bridge
Human descriptions → operational definitions → candidate algorithms → measurable tests; AND computational models → predicted observations → comparison with human biological/psychological phenomena → revised definitions. Both directions must retain provenance, uncertainty, and level-of-description boundaries.

### Candidate biological mapping (interpretive hypotheses)
| Biological/psychological system | Operational observable | Candidate computational mechanism | Falsification or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homeostasis | controlled variable, perturbation, recovery | negative-feedback controller | test recovery outside trained perturbations; avoid equating metaphor with mechanism |
| Development / differentiation | lineage, timing, regulatory state | state transition / constraint propagation | gene regulation and environmental inputs cannot be collapsed to a single deterministic sequence |
| Sensorimotor learning | prediction error, adaptation | online model update | separate correlation from causal intervention |
| Memory reconsolidation | retrieval-dependent behavioral changes | versioned state update with provenance | no assumption that biological memory is a database |
| Metacognition | confidence calibration and error detection | confidence estimate + independent evaluator | require calibration under distribution shift |
| Social learning | observed imitation and communication | multi-agent information transfer | guard against anthropomorphism |

### Proposed experiment INF-STAIR-E01
1. Choose one system (homeostasis first) and collect time-series of input, output, disturbance, and recovery.
2. Write plain-language definitions for identity, error, target, action, adaptation, and interpretation.
3. Formalize a minimal controller and a nonfeedback baseline with matched capacity.
4. Freeze tests: recovery time, stability, calibration, perturbation generalization, and evidence provenance.
5. Compare measured observations with model predictions; label unsupported analogies explicitly.
6. Reverse-translate model behavior into human-readable explanations and test whether a second reader can predict failures from those explanations.
