## Suggested reader note — subject to Timothy's approval

> **Displayed precision and alternative calculations.** The two source tables print masses to 0.01 gram, but do not specify whether these values are direct measurements, rounded calculations or truncated calculations. Using the table's stated 13.7-gram base, Dataset B's eight printed masses match a calculation **truncated** to 0.01 gram (8/8). A nearest-0.01-gram calculation instead matches **7/8** with ties-to-even or **6/8** with ties-away-from-zero. For example, 1/16 of 13.7 grams is 0.85625 grams (printed as 0.85 grams); 1/4 is 3.425 grams (printed as 3.42 grams). These agreements do not establish how the source figures were produced or independently validate historical measurements. Dataset A's printed percentage deviations cannot all be reproduced from its displayed masses alone; its original precision and calculation method are not supplied. All source values remain unchanged, and the conflicting uses of *Gerah* (1/8 versus 1/16 unit) remain unresolved.

**Editorial precision:** "truncated" here describes a hypothetical numerical transformation that reproduces the printed values, **not** a finding that the author deliberately truncated data. Prefer the exact phrase "compatible with truncation" in captions and marketing descriptions. Do not use "verified archaeological precision", "forensic proof", "historical measurement confirmed", or a statistical confidence claim without independent evidence.
