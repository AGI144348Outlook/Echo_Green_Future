## Dataset B — eight source rows

Theoretical mass = 13.7g × (printed ratio / 16); the comparison column uses a *declared* nearest-0.01g ties-to-even rule. The source did not document a rounding rule, so this is a computational illustration, not an attribution of method to its author.

| Row | Printed ratio | Printed mass (g) | Theoretical mass (g) | Half-even at 0.01g | Disposition | Printed relation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 0.85 | 0.85625 | 0.86 | REVIEW | Trace/Gerah Base |
| 2 | 2 | 1.71 | 1.7125 | 1.71 | MATCH | Matches "Small Scrap" |
| 3 | 4 | 3.42 | 3.425 | 3.42 | MATCH | Matches "Silver Droplet" |
| 4 | 8 | 6.85 | 6.85 | 6.85 | MATCH | The Beqa Invariant |
| 5 | 16 | 13.70 | 13.7 | 13.70 | MATCH | The Sanctuary Shekel |
| 6 | 32 | 27.40 | 27.4 | 27.40 | MATCH | Commercial Standard |
| 7 | 80 | 68.50 | 68.5 | 68.50 | MATCH | Matches "Coiled Wire" |
| 8 | 160 | 137.00 | 137.0 | 137.00 | MATCH | Matches "Ingot Slice" |

**Observed:** 7/8 source masses equal the declared half-even rounding. At ratio 1 (1/16 unit), theoretical mass is **0.85625g**, which rounds to **0.86g** by this rule; source prints **0.85g**. At ratio 4 (1/4 unit), theoretical mass is exactly **3.425g**, a tie at two decimals; ties-to-even gives **3.42g**, while ties-away-from-zero gives **3.43g**. This is why the chosen rounding convention must be disclosed rather than silently presumed.

## Terminology conflict and editorial gates

Dataset A labels **0.125 U (1/8)** as *Gerah (Metric Sub-unit)*; Dataset B labels **1/16 U** as *Trace/Gerah Base*. Both source usages are retained without choosing one as authoritative. A future glossary should show both with a visible “terminology unresolved” note unless Timothy supplies an editorial ruling and supporting evidence.
