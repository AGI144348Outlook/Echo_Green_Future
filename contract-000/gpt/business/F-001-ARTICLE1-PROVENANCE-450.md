## Editorial findings (observation vs interpretation)
1. **SOURCE-OBSERVED discrepancy, not resolved:** Yeivin's 1987 account says **62 lb ≈ 28.12 kg**, while Eshel's 2025 synthesis says **~23 kg**. Article 1 says **over 26 kg**. These may reflect different weighings, subsets, or revisions; **do not silently select one** or claim the others are necessarily false. Attribute each estimate to its source and obtain the specialist catalogue before final wording.
2. **SOURCE-OBSERVED source gap:** Dataset A is labelled recalculated and lacks archaeological object-level locators. It is **derived/unverified** for the purpose of a claim that six measured artifacts prove a historical standard. The hoard's existence is independently supported; the six specific masses are not yet independently authenticated.
3. **INFERRED temporal risk:** The manuscript's second-millennium trade narrative is not directly evidenced by an Iron Age hoard whose published dating itself is debated. Any claimed Indus-to-Eshtemoa transmission needs its own dated intermediate evidence.
4. **UNVERIFIED:** Mari A.432 and Ugarit RS 15.10 are claimed to quote Meluhha weights and conversion disputes. No edition, line, transliteration, or translation was obtained in this run. Failure to locate the claimed passage in a bounded search **does not prove nonexistence**. Hold the assertion pending exact primary edition and line references.
5. **INFERRED methodological consequence:** Run449 demonstrated the 11.4g comparison grid is exactly 6/5 of the 13.7g grid (effective 13.68g). Without independently measured artifact weights and a preregistered admissible fraction set, close fit cannot discriminate historical systems.
