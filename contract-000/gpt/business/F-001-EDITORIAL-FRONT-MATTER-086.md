## Reader's note: hypothesis, not established history

The TAM Protocol brings together Timothy's investigations of ancient weight systems, inscriptions, administrative records and possible cultural transmission. Its central 13.7-gram invariant is a **proposed interpretive model**, not an independently demonstrated universal standard. The original repository contains hypotheses about Indus signs, Levantine weights, chronology and biological markers. Those connections must not be presented as established archaeological consensus without primary evidence, dating, independent replication and engagement with competing explanations.

This edition is intended to make the proposal **auditable**. A calculation that follows from a chosen baseline is different from proof that the baseline was used by an ancient society. A resemblance between ratios is different from evidence of contact. A named artifact is different from a verified excavation record. The book should preserve these distinctions on every page.

## What a reader should be able to reproduce

1. Identify the original source file and its version for each table or quotation.
2. Recalculate each conversion from the reported measurements, stating the unit, denominator, rounding method and uncertainty.
3. Separate **measured values** from **normalizations** and from **hypothetical correspondences**.
4. Follow every factual historical claim to a checkable publication, museum catalog or excavation report. Where no source is supplied, the statement remains an open claim, not evidence.
5. Compare the proposed explanation against alternatives: regional standards, ordinary trade variation, coincidence, selective sampling and later cultural reinterpretation.

The tables in the source repository are working datasets. Their numerical consistency is under editorial review; neither the existence of a table nor a computed percentage establishes provenance, sample representativeness or historical causation.

## Evidence labels used in this edition

- **SOURCE TRANSCRIPTION:** wording or numbers preserved from Timothy's source repository; not independently verified.
- **CALCULATION:** a reproducible transformation of explicitly named inputs; method and rounding rule shown.
- **DOCUMENTED EVIDENCE:** a claim accompanied by a traceable primary or scholarly reference, checked against the claim's actual wording.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** an explanation proposed for testing, with alternatives and potential falsifiers.
- **UNVERIFIED / EDITORIAL HOLD:** a claim without sufficient supporting evidence or with unresolved numerical, provenance or rights questions.

A statement such as “99.9% accuracy” requires a defined task, sample size, evaluation set, uncertainty interval and independent validation before it may appear as an empirical result. An assertion described as “Geophysical Fact” needs equally specific supporting records. Until then, such phrases must be quoted as unverified original claims or replaced with appropriately conditional language after Timothy and Claude review.
